做题要求
  1. 今天三种题型：段落信息匹配（1–5）、YES/NO/NOT GIVEN（6–9，看作者观点）、摘要填空（10–14，每空只填一个词）。
  2. 段落信息匹配：题目考的是细节，不是主旨。先抓题目里的关键词（printing error、two different times），再扫原文找同义替换。同一个字母可以用不止一次。
  3. YES/NO/NOT GIVEN：问的是作者的看法。原文观点相同选 YES，相反选 NO，原文没提或没比较选 NOT GIVEN。别用自己的常识推断。
  4. 摘要填空：摘要顺序基本跟原文顺序一致，先定位 E 段，往后读。看空格前后要什么词性，直接抄原文，拼写和单复数错都算错。
  5. 点“检查答案”，错题写错因：同义替换没认出 / NO 和 NOT GIVEN 混了 / 拼写或单复数错 / 定位错段。做完点“显示全文翻译”对照，再挑 10 个虚线词记到底部。

Keeping the Same Time: How the Railways Gave Us Standard Time

💡 点击或悬停虚线词看中文；点击段落字母（A–G）可单独切换该段翻译

A Before the nineteenth century, every town kept its own time. Noon was the moment when the sun was highest in the sky, and because the sun appears to看起来 move from east to west, noon arrived a few minutes earlier in eastern towns than in western ones. In Britain, the difference between London and Bristol was about ten minutes, and between London and the far west of Cornwall it was more than twenty. For most people, this hardly mattered几乎无关紧要. A journey between two towns took a day or more by horse or coach, and nobody worried that their watch was a few minutes out when they arrived. Local time was set by a church clock or a sundial日晷, and it was accurate enough for daily life.

【A 段译文】19世纪以前，每个城镇都用自己的时间。正午就是太阳升到最高的那一刻，而太阳看起来是从东往西移动的，所以东边城镇的正午要比西边城镇早几分钟到来。在英国，伦敦和布里斯托尔相差大约十分钟，伦敦和康沃尔最西端相差二十多分钟。对大多数人来说，这几乎无关紧要。两个城镇之间骑马或坐马车要走一天甚至更久，到了以后手表差几分钟，没人会在意。当地时间按教堂的钟或日晷来定，对日常生活来说已经够准了。

B The railways changed this completely. When trains began to carry passengers between cities in the 1830s and 1840s, a journey that had taken a day could be completed in a few hours, and the confusion混乱 caused by local time became obvious. Each station displayed its own time, so timetables had to list departures in the local time of each town, and passengers frequently missed their connections换乘（的车）. More seriously, railway companies were worried about accidents. Many lines had only a single track in places, and if two drivers had different ideas about the time, the result could be a collision相撞. It is fair to say that safety, rather than convenience, was the stronger motive动机 for change. In November 1840 the Great Western Railway decided to run all its trains according to London time, and other companies soon followed. By 1847 most British railways had adopted采用 what became known as "railway time", based on the clock at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich.

【B 段译文】铁路彻底改变了这一切。19世纪30到40年代，火车开始在城市之间载客，原来要走一天的路几个小时就能到，当地时间带来的混乱一下子暴露出来。每个车站显示自己的时间，所以时刻表得按各个城镇的当地时间列出发车时刻，乘客经常赶不上换乘。更严重的是，铁路公司担心出事故。很多线路有些路段只有一条轨道，如果两个司机对时间的理解不一样，就可能撞车。可以说，推动改变的更大动力是安全，而不是方便。1840年11月，大西部铁路决定所有列车都按伦敦时间运行，其他公司很快跟进。到1847年，英国大多数铁路都采用了后来被称为“铁路时间”的标准，以格林尼治皇家天文台的时钟为准。

C Not everyone welcomed the change. In many towns, people regarded local time as natural, and they resented反感；不满 being told by a company in London when it was noon. Some town councils refused to alter their public clocks, and for a number of years two times existed side by side并存. The Corn Exchange in Bristol installed安装 a clock with two minute hands, one showing local time and the other showing London time, and it can still be seen today. However, the objections were not simply stubbornness固执. Courts and contracts合同 still used local time, so it was unclear which time counted when, for example, a legal document had to be delivered "by noon". The matter was only settled解决 in 1880, when Parliament made Greenwich Mean Time the legal time throughout Great Britain.

【C 段译文】并不是所有人都欢迎这个变化。在很多城镇，人们觉得当地时间是天经地义的，很反感由伦敦的一家公司来告诉他们什么时候是正午。有些镇议会拒绝调整公共时钟，于是好些年里两种时间同时存在。布里斯托尔的谷物交易所装了一个有两根分针的钟，一根显示当地时间，一根显示伦敦时间，至今还能看到。不过，反对意见并不只是固执。法院和合同仍然用当地时间，所以比如一份法律文件必须“在正午前”送到，到底按哪个时间算就说不清了。这个问题直到1880年才解决，那一年议会把格林尼治平时定为全英国的法定时间。

D In North America, the problem was on a much larger scale规模大得多. The United States stretches across more than fifty degrees of longitude经度, and in the 1870s American railways were using around fifty different local times. A station used by several companies might have several clocks, each showing a different time. A number of people put forward提出 solutions. Charles Dowd, a school principal from New York State, proposed dividing the country into time zones one hour apart, and the Canadian railway engineer Sandford Fleming went further, suggesting a system of twenty-four zones covering the whole world. Fleming's interest is said to have begun in 1876, when he missed a train in Ireland because the timetable had printed "p.m." instead of "a.m.".

【D 段译文】在北美，这个问题的规模要大得多。美国横跨五十多个经度，19世纪70年代美国的铁路用着大约五十种不同的当地时间。一个几家公司共用的车站可能挂着好几个钟，每个钟显示的时间都不一样。不少人提出了解决办法。纽约州的一位校长查尔斯·多德提议把全国划分成相差一小时的几个时区，加拿大铁路工程师桑福德·弗莱明更进一步，提出覆盖全世界的二十四时区体系。据说弗莱明对这个问题的兴趣始于1876年，那次他在爱尔兰没赶上火车，因为时刻表把“下午”错印成了“上午”。

E In the end, however, it was the railway companies rather than governments that acted. On 18 November 1883, American and Canadian railways introduced four standard time zones across the continent. At noon, clocks in many places were stopped or moved back so that they matched the new standard, and some towns experienced noon twice; newspapers called it "the day of two noons". The change was made without any law, and although most cities quickly accepted railway time for ordinary use, a few places held out坚持抵制 for years. Standard time did not become law across the United States until 1918.

【E 段译文】不过最后采取行动的是铁路公司，而不是政府。1883年11月18日，美国和加拿大的铁路在整个大陆推行了四个标准时区。正午时分，很多地方的钟被停下或往回拨，以便和新标准对齐，有些城镇过了两次正午，报纸称之为“有两个正午的一天”。这次改变没有任何法律依据，虽然大多数城市很快就在日常生活中接受了铁路时间，但也有少数地方坚持抵制了好几年。直到1918年，标准时间才在全美成为法律。

F A worldwide system required international agreement on where the zones should begin. In 1884, delegates代表 from twenty-five countries met in Washington, D.C., for the International Meridian Conference and voted to make the line passing through Greenwich the world's prime meridian本初子午线, the starting point for measuring longitude. The choice was partly practical: by then, most of the world's ships already used charts based on Greenwich. The vote was not unanimous全体一致的, however. San Domingo voted against, while France and Brazil abstained弃权, and France continued to use Paris time for its own purposes until 1911.

【F 段译文】全球统一的体系需要各国就时区从哪里开始达成一致。1884年，二十五个国家的代表在华盛顿特区召开国际子午线会议，投票决定把穿过格林尼治的那条线定为世界本初子午线，也就是计算经度的起点。这个选择部分是出于实际考虑：当时世界上大多数船只已经在用以格林尼治为基准的海图。不过投票并不是全票通过。圣多明各投了反对票，法国和巴西弃权，法国在国内一直用巴黎时间，直到1911年。

G Today it is difficult to imagine a world without standard time, yet the system is less tidy整齐的 than its inventors intended. Many time zones follow political borders政治边界 rather than lines of longitude. China, which is wide enough for five zones, uses a single time across the whole country, while a few countries use zones that differ from their neighbours by half an hour. Daylight saving夏令时, introduced in many countries during the twentieth century, adds a further complication twice a year. Even so, the basic principle that the railway engineers established, that a whole region should share one clock, is now so widely accepted that most people never think about it.

【G 段译文】如今很难想象一个没有标准时间的世界，但这个体系并不像发明者设想的那么整齐。很多时区是按政治边界而不是经线划分的。中国的宽度够分五个时区，却全国统一用一个时间，还有少数国家用的时区和邻国相差半小时。很多国家在20世纪引入了夏令时，每年两次调钟又多了一层麻烦。尽管如此，当年铁路工程师确立的基本原则，也就是一整个地区共用一个时钟，如今已被普遍接受，大多数人根本不会去想它。

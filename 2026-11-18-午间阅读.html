Waiting in Line: The Hidden Science of Queues

💡 点击或悬停虚线词看中文；点击段落字母（A–G）可单独切换该段翻译

A Most of us spend a surprising amount of our lives waiting in line. One frequently quoted estimate suggests that the average person in a developed country spends between one and two years of their life in queues, whether at supermarket checkouts, bank counters or airport security. Because queues are so common, they are usually treated as an annoying but unavoidable不可避免的 part of modern life. Yet for more than a century, mathematicians, engineers and psychologists have studied them seriously, and their findings have changed the way that shops, hospitals and theme parks are designed.

【A 段译文】我们大多数人一生中花在排队上的时间多得惊人。一个经常被引用的估算说，发达国家的普通人一生要在队伍里度过一到两年，不管是在超市收银台、银行柜台还是机场安检。因为排队太常见了，人们通常把它当成现代生活里烦人但躲不开的一部分。然而一百多年来，数学家、工程师和心理学家一直在认真研究排队，他们的发现改变了商店、医院和主题公园的设计方式。

B The scientific study of queues began not in a shop but in a telephone exchange电话交换局. In the early 1900s, the Copenhagen Telephone Company wanted to know how many operators接线员 and lines it needed so that callers would not have to wait too long to be connected. Too few, and customers would complain; too many, and the company would be paying for equipment that stood idle闲置着. A Danish engineer, Agner Krarup Erlang, approached the problem mathematically. In a paper published in 1909, he showed that although individual calls arrived at random随机地, the overall pattern of calls could be predicted with surprising accuracy. His work became the foundation基础 of what is now called queuing theory, a branch of mathematics that is used today to plan everything from call centres to computer networks.

【B 段译文】对排队的科学研究不是从商店开始的，而是从电话交换局开始的。20世纪初，哥本哈根电话公司想知道需要多少接线员和线路，才能让打电话的人不用等太久就能接通。太少了顾客会抱怨，太多了公司就得为闲置的设备花钱。丹麦工程师阿格纳·克拉鲁普·埃尔朗用数学方法来解决这个问题。他在1909年发表的论文中证明，虽然单个电话是随机打进来的，但电话的整体规律却能被相当准确地预测。他的研究成了今天所说的排队论的基础，这个数学分支如今被用来规划从呼叫中心到计算机网络的各种系统。

C One of the most important lessons of queuing theory is that waiting times do not rise steadily as a system gets busier. When a checkout is working at half its capacity容量；（最大）能力, queues are short and they clear quickly. But as the number of customers approaches接近 the maximum that the staff can serve, waiting times begin to grow very sharply急剧地. A shop where the cashiers are busy 95 per cent of the time may have queues several times longer than one where they are busy 80 per cent of the time, even though the difference in the number of customers is small. For this reason, managers who try to save money by keeping every member of staff constantly occupied often find that they have created long delays and dissatisfied不满意的 customers.

【C 段译文】排队论最重要的结论之一是：系统越来越忙时，等待时间并不是平稳上升的。收银台只用了一半能力时，队伍很短，也散得很快。但当顾客人数接近员工能服务的上限时，等待时间就开始急剧增长。收银员 95% 的时间都在忙的商店，队伍可能比 80% 时间在忙的商店长好几倍，尽管两者的顾客人数相差不大。所以，想靠让每个员工一刻不停地忙来省钱的经理，常常会发现自己造成了长时间的拖延和一肚子不满的顾客。

D The design of the queue itself also matters. In many supermarkets, each checkout has its own line, and customers must choose which one to join. This arrangement often feels unfair, because a single slow transaction交易；一笔业务, such as a customer who wants to pay with coins or return an item, can hold up耽搁；拖住 everyone behind them while the neighbouring line moves on. Many banks and post offices therefore use a single line that winds back and forth来回蜿蜒 and feeds several counters, with the person at the front going to whichever counter becomes free next. Studies suggest that the average wait in such a system is not always much shorter, but the waiting times are more predictable可预测的, and customers are far less likely to see someone who arrived after them being served first.

【D 段译文】队伍本身怎么设计也很重要。很多超市每个收银台各排一队，顾客得自己选排哪一队。这种安排常常让人觉得不公平，因为一笔慢吞吞的交易，比如有顾客要用硬币付款或者退货，就会耽搁后面所有人，而旁边那队却一直往前走。所以很多银行和邮局用一条来回蜿蜒的单一队伍，通向好几个柜台，排在最前面的人去下一个空出来的柜台。研究表明，这种方式的平均等待时间不一定短多少，但等待时间更可预测，顾客也很少会看到比自己晚来的人先被服务。

E Indeed, research suggests that how a wait feels may be more important than how long it actually lasts. In an influential有影响力的 article published in 1985, the American business writer David Maister set out阐述；提出 a number of principles about the psychology of waiting. Among them were the ideas that unoccupied无所事事的 time feels longer than occupied time, that uncertain waits feel longer than known ones, and that unexplained waits feel longer than explained ones. Maister also argued that waiting alone feels longer than waiting in a group. A frequently told story from an airport in Houston, Texas, illustrates说明；举例证明 his first principle. Passengers complained about the time they spent waiting for their luggage, even though the delay was only about eight minutes. Rather than hiring more baggage handlers行李搬运员, the airport moved the arrival gates further from the baggage hall, so that passengers spent most of the time walking. The complaints almost disappeared, although the total time had hardly changed.

【E 段译文】事实上，研究表明，等待的感受可能比实际等了多久更重要。1985年，美国商业作家大卫·梅斯特发表了一篇很有影响力的文章，阐述了关于等待心理的几条原则。其中包括：无所事事的时间比有事做的时间感觉更长，不确定的等待比知道要等多久的感觉更长，没有解释的等待比有解释的感觉更长。梅斯特还认为，一个人等比一群人一起等感觉更长。德克萨斯州休斯敦一个机场的故事经常被人提起，正好说明了他的第一条原则。乘客抱怨等行李的时间太长，尽管其实只等了大约八分钟。机场没有多雇行李搬运员，而是把到达登机口挪得离行李大厅更远，让乘客把大部分时间花在走路上。投诉几乎消失了，虽然总时间几乎没变。

F Businesses have used these ideas in many ways. Theme parks are often considered the experts. Queues for popular rides are frequently designed so that visitors cannot see their full length, and screens, decorations and staff in costume give people something to look at. Some parks display estimated waiting times that are deliberately故意地 slightly longer than the real wait, so that visitors are pleasantly surprised感到惊喜 when they reach the front sooner than expected. Similarly, many restaurants and clinics now send a text message when a table or appointment is ready, allowing customers to wait somewhere more comfortable instead of standing in line.

【F 段译文】商家用各种方式运用了这些想法。主题公园常被认为是这方面的行家。热门项目的队伍往往被设计成游客看不到队尾，屏幕、装饰和穿着戏服的工作人员让人有东西可看。有些公园显示的预计等待时间故意比实际稍长一点，这样游客比预想更早排到前面时会感到惊喜。同样，现在很多餐厅和诊所会在座位或预约准备好时发短信，让顾客可以在更舒服的地方等，而不用站着排队。

G Not every attempt to reduce waiting has been successful, however. Self-service checkouts, for instance, were introduced partly to shorten queues in supermarkets, but some customers find them frustrating令人恼火的, particularly when the machine stops and they must wait for an assistant to help. There is also a question of fairness. Systems that allow people to pay extra to skip the line不排队直接进；插到前面, which are now common at airports and attractions景点, may make the experience better for some customers while making it worse for others. As one researcher has pointed out指出, a queue is not only a practical arrangement but also a small social system, and people care deeply about whether it treats everyone equally.

【G 段译文】不过，并不是所有减少等待的尝试都成功了。比如自助收银机，引进它的部分原因是缩短超市的队伍，但有些顾客觉得它让人很恼火，尤其是机器卡住、得等店员来帮忙的时候。还有一个公平问题。允许人们多付钱跳过排队的做法，如今在机场和景点很常见，它可能让一部分顾客体验更好，同时让另一部分顾客体验更差。正如一位研究者指出的，队伍不只是一种实用的安排，也是一个小小的社会系统，人们非常在意它是否对每个人一视同仁。

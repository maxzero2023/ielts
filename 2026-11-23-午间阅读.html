Tiny Forests: Can a City Grow a Woodland in a Car Park?

Read the text below and answer Questions 1–14.

💡 点击或悬停虚线词看中文；点击段落字母（A–G）可单独切换该段翻译

A In a car park on the edge of a city, a patch of ground about the size of a tennis court has been turned into something unexpected: a forest. Over the past decade, hundreds of these 'tiny forests' have appeared in schoolyards, beside motorways and on unused corners of housing estates住宅区 in Europe, India and elsewhere. They are planted using a technique named after the Japanese botanist植物学家 Akira Miyawaki, and their supporters say they offer a quick and simple way to bring nature back into crowded urban areas. Not everyone, however, is convinced.

【A 段译文】在城市边缘的一个停车场里，一块大约网球场大小的地被变成了一样出人意料的东西：一片森林。过去十年里，欧洲、印度和其他地方的校园里、高速公路旁、住宅区闲置的角落里出现了数百片这样的“迷你森林”。它们是用一种以日本植物学家宫胁昭命名的技术种出来的，支持者说，这是把大自然带回拥挤城区的一种又快又简单的办法。不过，并不是所有人都信服。

B Miyawaki spent many years surveying调查，勘测 the natural vegetation植被 of Japan. He noticed that many of the oldest and most varied woodlands had survived around temples and shrines, where trees had been protected for centuries. By contrast, much of the country's forest had been replaced by commercial plantations人工林 of a few fast-growing species such as cedar and pine. He concluded that these plantations were less able to withstand抵御，经受住 storms, fires and disease than the native woodland they had replaced, and he began to look for a way of recreating natural forest in far less time than nature alone would need.

【B 段译文】宫胁花了很多年调查日本的天然植被。他注意到，许多最古老、种类最丰富的林地都保存在寺庙和神社周围，那里的树木受了几百年的保护。相比之下，日本大部分森林已经被只种少数几种速生树（比如杉树和松树）的商业人工林取代了。他得出结论：这些人工林抵御风暴、火灾和病害的能力不如被它们取代的原生林，于是他开始寻找一种办法，用比大自然自己所需短得多的时间重建天然森林。

C The method has three main steps. First, researchers study the surrounding area to identify the plant species that would naturally grow there, typically thirty or more types of trees and shrubs灌木. Next, the soil is prepared: it is often dug to a depth of about one metre and mixed with organic material有机物 such as straw or compost so that roots can spread easily. Finally, young saplings树苗 are planted very close together, at a density密度 of around three per square metre, in a random arrangement rather than in neat rows. Because the plants compete for light, they grow upwards rapidly. Volunteers water and weed the site for the first two or three years, after which the forest is expected to look after itself.

【C 段译文】这个方法有三个主要步骤。第一步，研究人员考察周边地区，确定当地会自然生长的植物种类，通常是三十种或更多的乔木和灌木。第二步，整理土壤：一般要挖到大约一米深，混入稻草或堆肥之类的有机物，让根系容易伸展。最后，把小树苗种得非常密，每平方米大约三棵，而且随机排列，不排成整齐的行。因为植物之间争夺阳光，它们会快速向上长。头两三年由志愿者浇水除草，之后森林就应该能自己照顾自己了。

D Supporters make striking惊人的，显著的 claims. They say that a Miyawaki forest grows ten times faster than a conventionally planted one and becomes thirty times denser. Small studies have reported a sharp rise in the number of insect species within a few years of planting, and some city councils argue that tiny forests help to cool nearby streets in summer and absorb吸收 heavy rain that would otherwise flood the drains排水沟. Schools have found another advantage: pupils who help to plant and monitor a forest gain a hands-on亲身实践的 understanding of ecology生态学 that is difficult to teach in a classroom.

【D 段译文】支持者的说法很惊人。他们说宫胁式森林比常规种植的森林长得快十倍，密度高三十倍。一些小规模研究报告说，种下几年内昆虫种类数量大幅增加；有些市政府认为迷你森林能在夏天给附近街道降温，并吸收原本会让排水沟漫水的大雨。学校还发现了另一个好处：帮忙种树、观察森林的学生能亲身理解生态学，这在教室里很难教。

E Critics point out that many of these figures come from the organisations that promote the method rather than from independent research独立研究. Some foresters林业人员 argue that rapid early growth is hardly surprising, since any young tree grows quickly when it is given rich soil and regular watering, and that the real test is what happens after twenty or thirty years. In some places, a large proportion of the saplings have died once volunteers stopped caring for them, particularly where the chosen species were not well suited to the local climate. Dense planting, the critics add, means that many trees will inevitably不可避免地 be crowded out被挤掉, which raises the cost of each tree that survives.

【E 段译文】批评者指出，这些数字很多来自推广这个方法的机构，而不是独立研究。一些林业人员认为，早期长得快一点也不奇怪，因为任何小树只要有肥沃的土壤和规律的浇水都会长得快，真正的考验是二三十年后会怎样。在有些地方，志愿者一停止照料，很大一部分树苗就死了，尤其是选的树种不太适应当地气候的地方。批评者还说，种得这么密意味着很多树不可避免地会被挤掉，这就抬高了每一棵存活下来的树的成本。

F There is also debate about value for money. Because the soil preparation is labour-intensive劳动密集的，费人工的 and so many saplings are needed, a tiny forest can cost several times more per square metre than ordinary tree planting. Defenders reply that comparing the two is misleading误导性的. In a dense city, open land is scarce稀缺的, and a technique that produces a large amount of greenery on a small plot may be the only realistic option. A row of street trees and a tiny forest, they suggest, do different jobs and should not be judged by the same measure.

【F 段译文】关于值不值这笔钱也有争论。因为整土很费人工，又需要那么多树苗，迷你森林每平方米的成本可能是普通植树的好几倍。支持者回应说，这样比较有误导性。在密集的城市里，空地很稀缺，一种能在一小块地上种出大量绿色的技术，可能是唯一现实的选择。他们认为，一排行道树和一片迷你森林作用不同，不应该用同一把尺子衡量。

G What most observers agree on is that tiny forests are no substitute替代品 for protecting existing woodland, which stores far more carbon and supports far more wildlife than any newly planted patch. Several universities have now begun long-term monitoring of urban tiny forests, measuring growth, survival and biodiversity生物多样性 year by year, but their results will not be known for some time. In the meantime, perhaps the greatest achievement of the movement is social rather than ecological: it has given thousands of city residents a reason to get their hands dirty亲自动手, and a small piece of wild nature that they feel belongs to them.

【G 段译文】大多数观察者都同意的一点是：迷你森林代替不了对现有林地的保护，现有林地储存的碳、养活的野生动物都远远多于任何新种的一小片林子。现在有几所大学已经开始长期监测城市里的迷你森林，逐年测量生长、存活率和生物多样性，但结果要过一段时间才能知道。在此之前，这场运动最大的成就也许是社会层面的，而不是生态层面的：它给了成千上万城市居民一个亲自动手的理由，以及一小块他们觉得属于自己的野生自然。

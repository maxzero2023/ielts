来源：自编练习。官方免费 G 类样题已经用完，所以按 G 类 Section 3 的风格自己写（一篇较长的科普历史类文章：制冷和冰箱的历史），题型和难度对标真题，答案和解析为自编。今天是第 3 周第 2 天，基础期，不限时精读，参考用时 20 到 25 分钟。

做题要求
  1. 今天三种题型：段落信息匹配（1–6）、YES / NO / NOT GIVEN 作者观点判断（7–10）、选项框摘要填空（11–14）。
  2. 信息匹配：题目是细节不是主旨，先圈题目关键词，再按段扫读找同义替换。真题里同一段可以选多次，有的段可能不选。
  3. YNNG 和上周的 TFNG 不同：问的是作者的观点。原文是“批评者认为”而作者不同意时，要按作者的立场判断。原文没说就是 NOT GIVEN，别用常识补。
  4. 摘要填空：先读摘要定位到哪一段，再看空格前后判断词性（人、物、职业），最后从选项框里挑同义的词。8 个选项只用 4 个。
  5. 点“检查答案”，错题写错因：同义替换没认出 / 双重否定读反了 / 把别人的观点当成作者观点 / 用常识脑补。做完点“显示全文翻译”对照，再挑 10 个虚线词记到底部。

Keeping Things Cool: A Short History of Refrigeration

💡 点击或悬停虚线词看中文；点击段落字母（A–F）可单独切换该段翻译

A For most of human history, keeping food fresh was a constant struggle. People relied on methods such as salting, smoking, drying and pickling醋渍；腌制, all of which changed the taste and texture of what they ate. From the seventeenth century, wealthy families in Europe began to build ice houses: brick buildings with thick walls, often partly underground, into which blocks of ice cut from frozen lakes were packed during the winter. If the ice house was well built, the ice could last until late summer. However, building and filling one was expensive, so for ordinary people fresh food in hot weather remained a luxury奢侈品 they could not afford.

【A 段译文】在人类历史的大部分时间里，保持食物新鲜一直是件很费劲的事。人们靠盐腌、烟熏、晒干和醋渍等方法，而这些方法都会改变食物的味道和口感。从17世纪起，欧洲的富裕家庭开始建造冰窖：这是一种墙壁很厚的砖砌建筑，常常有一部分埋在地下，冬天人们把从结冰湖面上切下来的冰块堆进去。冰窖建得好的话，冰可以一直保存到夏末。不过建造和装满一个冰窖都很贵，所以对普通人来说，热天吃到新鲜食物仍是一种负担不起的奢侈。

B In the early nineteenth century, a Boston businessman named Frederic Tudor had an idea that many people thought was absurd荒唐的: he would sell natural ice to hot countries. In 1806 he sent a shipload of ice to the Caribbean island of Martinique. Much of it melted during the voyage航行, and the venture（有风险的）生意 lost money. Tudor, however, refused to give up. He experimented with different ways of storing his cargo and found that packing the ice in sawdust锯末, a cheap waste product from timber mills, greatly reduced melting. By the 1830s his ships were carrying ice as far as India. In the writer’s view, it was Tudor’s persistence坚持；毅力, rather than any new technology, that explains his eventual success. Ice harvesting soon became a major winter industry in New England, employing thousands of workers.

【B 段译文】19世纪初，波士顿一位名叫弗雷德里克·图德的商人有个很多人觉得荒唐的想法：把天然冰卖到炎热的国家去。1806年，他把一整船冰运到加勒比海的马提尼克岛。很多冰在航行途中就化掉了，这次生意亏了钱。可图德不肯放弃。他试验了各种储存货物的方法，发现用锯末包冰能大大减少融化，而锯末只是木材厂便宜的废料。到19世纪30年代，他的船已经把冰运到了遥远的印度。在笔者看来，图德最终能成功，靠的是他的坚持，而不是什么新技术。采冰很快成了新英格兰地区冬季的一大产业，雇用了成千上万的工人。

C Yet the natural ice trade had serious weaknesses. It depended entirely on the weather: a mild温和的（不冷的） winter meant that lakes did not freeze thickly enough, which led to shortages短缺 and sharp rises in price. There were also worries about hygiene卫生. As cities grew, ice was increasingly cut from rivers and ponds that were polluted by waste, and doctors warned that contaminated被污染的 ice could spread disease. These warnings were not without foundation并非没有根据, since several outbreaks（疾病）暴发 of illness were later linked to dirty ice. Such problems encouraged inventors to search for a way of making ice artificially, whatever the season.

【C 段译文】然而，天然冰贸易有严重的弱点。它完全看天吃饭：暖冬时湖面冻得不够厚，就会导致冰块短缺、价格飙升。另外还有卫生方面的担忧。随着城市扩张，冰越来越多地取自被污水污染的河流和池塘，医生警告说受污染的冰可能传播疾病。这些警告并非没有根据，因为后来好几次疾病暴发都被证实和不干净的冰有关。这些问题促使发明家们去寻找一种不论什么季节都能人工制冰的方法。

D The science behind artificial cooling is simple: when a liquid evaporates蒸发, it absorbs吸收 heat from its surroundings. As early as 1755, the Scottish professor William Cullen demonstrated that he could produce a small amount of ice by causing a liquid called ether to evaporate in a vacuum真空, but his experiment was never developed into a useful machine. A century later, James Harrison, a Scottish-born printer living in Australia, noticed that the ether he used to clean metal type made the metal feel very cold. He went on to build one of the first practical ice-making machines, which was quickly bought by breweries啤酒厂 that needed to keep beer cool in the Australian heat. In 1873 Harrison attempted to ship frozen meat to Britain, but the cooling system failed and the cargo spoiled变质；坏掉. Just seven years later, however, a ship called the Strathleven arrived in London from Australia with its frozen beef and lamb in perfect condition.

【D 段译文】人工制冷背后的科学原理很简单：液体蒸发时会从周围吸收热量。早在1755年，苏格兰教授威廉·卡伦就演示过，让一种叫乙醚的液体在真空中蒸发，可以制出少量的冰，但他的实验从来没有发展成实用的机器。一个世纪后，生于苏格兰、住在澳大利亚的印刷工詹姆斯·哈里森注意到，他用来清洗金属铅字的乙醚会让金属摸起来非常冰。他随后造出了最早的实用制冰机之一，很快就被啤酒厂买走，因为在澳大利亚的炎热天气里，啤酒厂需要给啤酒保冷。1873年，哈里森尝试把冷冻肉运到英国，但制冷系统出了故障，货物全坏了。然而仅仅七年之后，一艘名叫斯特拉斯利文号的船从澳大利亚抵达伦敦，船上的冷冻牛肉和羊肉完好无损。

E Refrigerators for the home appeared in the early twentieth century, but the first models used toxic有毒的 gases such as ammonia and sulphur dioxide, and leaks occasionally caused deaths. In 1930 chemists developed a new group of gases, known as CFCs, which were not poisonous and did not catch fire. Fridges using CFCs were considered completely safe, and they spread rapidly: by the 1950s most households in the United States owned one. Decades later, however, scientists discovered that CFCs were damaging the ozone layer臭氧层, which protects the Earth from harmful radiation. Under an international agreement signed in 1987, these gases were gradually phased out逐步淘汰 and replaced with alternatives.

【E 段译文】家用冰箱出现在20世纪初，但最早的型号用的是氨和二氧化硫这类有毒气体，偶尔会因泄漏而致人死亡。1930年，化学家研制出一类新气体，叫作氟氯化碳（CFC），它既无毒也不易燃。使用氟氯化碳的冰箱被认为完全安全，于是迅速普及：到20世纪50年代，美国大多数家庭都有了冰箱。然而几十年后，科学家发现氟氯化碳正在破坏臭氧层，而臭氧层保护地球免受有害辐射。根据1987年签署的一项国际协议，这些气体被逐步淘汰，换成了替代品。

F It is hard to exaggerate夸大 how much refrigeration has changed the way we live. Meat, fish, dairy products奶制品 and fresh vegetables can now be transported across the world and stored for weeks. Instead of visiting a market every day, many families do one large shop a week at a supermarket, and cases of food poisoning have fallen considerably. Some critics argue that we have lost our connection with the seasons, since strawberries are now on sale in the middle of winter. To the writer, this seems a small price to pay值得付出的小代价 for a safer and more varied diet. The real challenge today is energy: cooling already uses a large share of the world’s electricity, and demand is rising fast in hot countries, so making refrigerators more efficient has become an urgent priority当务之急.

【F 段译文】制冷对我们生活方式的改变之大，怎么说都不为过。肉、鱼、奶制品和新鲜蔬菜如今可以运往世界各地，并保存好几个星期。很多家庭不再每天去市场，而是每周去超市大采购一次，食物中毒的病例也大大减少。有批评者认为，我们已经失去了和季节的联系，因为隆冬时节也能买到草莓。在笔者看来，为了更安全、更丰富的饮食，这点代价微不足道。如今真正的挑战是能源：制冷已经占了全世界很大一部分用电量，而炎热国家的需求还在快速增长，所以提高冰箱的能效已经成为当务之急。

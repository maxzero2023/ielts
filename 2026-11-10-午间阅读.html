做题要求
  1. 今天三种题型：段落标题匹配（1–6）、人物特征匹配（7–10）、句子填空（11–14，每空不超过两个词）。
  2. 标题匹配：先读标题列表，再每段读首句和转折词（however、on the other hand）后面的句子，抓全段主旨。只说中某个细节的标题通常是干扰项。9 个标题只用 6 个。
  3. 人物匹配：先在原文里把 5 个人名圈出来，再看题目里的动词短语找同义替换。一个选项可能不选。
  4. 句子填空：先定位到 G 段，看空格前后要名词还是动词，直接抄原文的词，拼写错就算错。
  5. 点“检查答案”，错题写错因：把细节当主旨 / 同义替换没认出 / 超字数或拼写错 / 人物搞混。做完点“显示全文翻译”对照，再挑 10 个虚线词记到底部。

Under Cover: A Short History of the Umbrella

💡 点击或悬停虚线词看中文；点击段落字母（A–G）可单独切换该段翻译

A The umbrella is so ordinary today that it is easy to forget how special it once was. The earliest umbrellas, which appear in carvings and paintings from ancient Egypt, Assyria and China, were not designed to keep off the rain at all. They were sunshades遮阳伞, and they were held by servants over the heads of kings, nobles and priests. In many of these societies, the number of umbrellas a person was entitled to有资格（享有）, or the colour of the cloth, showed his position in society, and an ordinary person who carried one could be severely严厉地 punished. For thousands of years, therefore, the umbrella was less a practical tool than a sign of authority权力；权威.

【A 段译文】如今雨伞再普通不过，人们很容易忘了它曾经多么特别。最早的伞出现在古埃及、亚述和中国的雕刻和绘画里，根本不是用来挡雨的。它们是遮阳伞，由仆人举在国王、贵族和祭司的头上。在这些社会里，一个人有资格用几把伞、伞布是什么颜色，都显示着他的社会地位，普通人要是打伞，可能会受到严厉惩罚。所以几千年来，伞与其说是实用工具，不如说是权力的象征。

B When umbrellas reached Western Europe in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, they were mainly used by wealthy women as protection from the sun, and they quickly became fashionable accessories配饰. Men, on the other hand, were reluctant不情愿的 to be seen with one. A man who carried an umbrella was considered weak or effeminate女里女气的, and in Britain he might also be suspected of being too poor to own a carriage. A gentleman caught in the rain was expected to put up with忍受 getting wet, or to wait for a coach. Some coffee houses kept an umbrella by the door for customers to borrow on the short walk to their carriage, but it was rarely carried in the street.

【B 段译文】16、17世纪雨伞传到西欧时，主要是有钱的女性拿来遮阳，很快就成了时髦的配饰。而男人却不愿意被人看到拿着伞。拿伞的男人会被认为软弱或者女里女气，在英国还可能被怀疑穷得连马车都没有。绅士遇到下雨，按规矩要么忍着被淋湿，要么等马车来。有些咖啡馆在门口放一把伞，借给客人走到马车那几步路用，但很少有人在街上拿着伞走。

C The person usually credited with被认为是……的功劳 changing this attitude is Jonas Hanway, a London merchant and writer who had travelled widely in Persia, where umbrellas were common. From the 1750s, Hanway carried an umbrella whenever he went out, regardless of不管；不顾 the jokes and insults directed at him. The most hostile敌对的 reaction came from the drivers of hired coaches, who depended on rainy days for much of their income and saw the umbrella as a threat to their livelihood生计; it is said that they shouted at him and even tried to drive their coaches through puddles to splash him. Hanway refused to change his habit, and by the time of his death in 1786, a growing number of London men had begun to follow his example.

【C 段译文】一般认为改变这种观念的人是乔纳斯·汉威，他是伦敦的商人兼作家，曾在波斯到处旅行，而那里打伞很常见。从18世纪50年代起，汉威每次出门都打着伞，不管别人怎么取笑和辱骂他。反应最敌对的是出租马车的车夫，他们很大一部分收入靠下雨天，把雨伞看成对自己生计的威胁；据说他们冲他大喊大叫，甚至故意把马车赶过水坑去溅他一身水。汉威就是不改这个习惯，到他1786年去世时，越来越多的伦敦男人已经开始学他了。

D Early rain umbrellas, however, were far from convenient. Their ribs were usually made of whalebone鲸须 or wood, and the cover was heavy cotton or silk treated with oil or wax to make it waterproof防水的. A large umbrella could weigh several kilograms, it was awkward笨拙的；不方便的 to open and close, and once wet it took a long time to dry. The breakthrough突破 came in 1852, when an English manufacturer, Samuel Fox, began making umbrella frames with ribs of thin, curved steel. Fox is said to have used surplus剩余的；过剩的 steel that had originally been produced for women's corsets. His frames were lighter, stronger and cheaper than whalebone, and within a few decades steel had almost completely replaced the older materials.

【D 段译文】不过早期的雨伞一点都不方便。伞骨一般用鲸须或木头做，伞面是厚棉布或丝绸，用油或蜡处理过才能防水。一把大伞能重好几公斤，开合很费劲，一旦淋湿要很久才干。突破出现在1852年，英国制造商塞缪尔·福克斯开始用细而弯曲的钢条做伞骨。据说福克斯用的是原本为女士紧身胸衣生产的剩余钢材。他做的伞架比鲸须的更轻、更结实也更便宜，几十年内钢材就几乎完全取代了老材料。

E Even with a steel frame, though, an umbrella remained a long object that had to be carried in the hand or hung on the arm. In 1928 a German engineering student, Hans Haupt, designed a telescopic可伸缩的 umbrella that could be folded down to a fraction of……的一小部分 its normal length. Haupt had been injured during the First World War and walked with a stick, which made holding both a stick and a full-sized umbrella difficult; his compact小巧的 design solved his own problem first. His folding umbrella could be slipped into a handbag or a coat pocket, and the idea was soon copied by manufacturers in other countries.

【E 段译文】不过就算有了钢骨，伞依然是个长条东西，得拿在手里或者挂在胳膊上。1928年，德国一名工科学生汉斯·豪普特设计出一种可伸缩的伞，能折到只有正常长度的一小截。豪普特在第一次世界大战中受过伤，走路要拄拐杖，所以同时拿拐杖和一把全尺寸的伞很困难；他的折叠设计首先解决了他自己的问题。他的折叠伞可以塞进手提包或外套口袋里，这个点子很快就被其他国家的制造商仿效。

F In the second half of the twentieth century, mass production大规模生产, mainly in Asia, made umbrellas cheaper than ever before. Synthetic合成的 fabrics such as nylon replaced cotton and silk, and frames were made from lighter metals and plastic. As a result, the umbrella changed from an object that people looked after for years, and had repaired when it broke, into one that is often thrown away after a single storm. Some traditional makers still exist; James Smith and Sons, a London shop founded in 1830, continues to make umbrellas by hand in the old way. For most people, however, an umbrella is now something to buy cheaply at a station kiosk小卖亭 when the weather turns bad.

【F 段译文】20世纪下半叶，大规模生产（主要在亚洲）让雨伞变得前所未有地便宜。尼龙之类的合成面料取代了棉布和丝绸，伞架也改用更轻的金属和塑料。结果，雨伞从一件人们会爱护好多年、坏了还会拿去修的东西，变成了常常用过一场暴风雨就扔掉的东西。一些传统制伞商仍然存在：1830年在伦敦开业的詹姆斯·史密斯父子店，至今还在用老办法手工做伞。但对大多数人来说，伞现在只是天变坏时在车站小卖部随手买的便宜货。

G This throwaway culture has created an environmental problem. Cheap umbrellas are difficult to recycle because they combine steel, plastic and fabric, and these different parts have to be separated by hand, which is rarely worth the cost. Designers have responded in several ways. Some now make umbrellas from a single material, such as recycled plastic, so that the whole object can be melted down at the end of its life. Others focus on durability耐用性, offering a repair service so that customers can keep the same umbrella for many years. A number of cities in Asia have also experimented with umbrella-sharing schemes, in which people borrow an umbrella at one station and leave it at another. These schemes have had mixed results好坏参半的结果, however, as large numbers of umbrellas were simply never returned.

【G 段译文】这种用完即扔的风气造成了环境问题。便宜的雨伞很难回收，因为它们混合了钢、塑料和布料，这些部件得靠人手拆开，而这样做通常不划算。设计师们用几种办法来应对。有的现在只用一种材料做伞，比如再生塑料，这样整把伞在报废时可以一起熔化回收。还有的注重耐用，提供维修服务，让顾客一把伞能用很多年。亚洲一些城市还试行了共享雨伞计划，人们在一个车站借伞，在另一个车站还。不过这些计划效果好坏参半，因为大量的伞借走后就再也没还回来。

The Return of the Night Train

Read the text below and answer Questions 1–14.

💡 点击或悬停虚线词看中文；点击段落字母（A–G）可单独切换该段翻译

A For much of the twentieth century, sleeper trains卧铺列车 were a familiar part of European travel. Passengers boarded in the evening, ate dinner in a dining car, slept in a narrow bunk（火车）铺位 and woke up in another country. By the early 2000s, however, many of these services had disappeared. Low-cost airlines could carry people between capital cities in two hours for less than the price of a train ticket, and national rail companies, under pressure to cut losses, withdrew撤下，停开 routes that had run for decades. In 2016, Germany's national operator ended its last overnight夜间的，过夜的 services, describing them as a business it could no longer afford.

【A 段译文】二十世纪的大部分时间里，卧铺列车是欧洲旅行很常见的一部分。乘客傍晚上车，在餐车吃晚饭，睡在窄窄的铺位上，醒来已经到了另一个国家。可是到了 2000 年代初，这类线路很多都消失了。廉价航空两个小时就能把人从一个首都送到另一个首都，票价还比火车票便宜；各国铁路公司在减亏压力下，撤掉了运营几十年的线路。2016 年，德国国家铁路公司停开了最后的夜间列车，说这是一门它再也负担不起的生意。

B Only a few years later, the picture looks very different. Austria's state railway, which took over several of the abandoned被放弃的 German routes, now runs a network of night trains linking cities such as Vienna, Munich, Zurich, Hamburg and Rome, and new services have started between Brussels and Berlin and between Paris and Vienna. Private companies have also entered the market, some of them start-ups funded by investors who believe that demand will keep growing. Several governments have offered subsidies补贴, arguing that night trains help them meet targets for reducing transport emissions排放.

【B 段译文】仅仅几年以后，情况就大不一样了。奥地利国家铁路接手了好几条被德国放弃的线路，现在运营着一个夜车网络，连接维也纳、慕尼黑、苏黎世、汉堡和罗马等城市；布鲁塞尔到柏林、巴黎到维也纳也开通了新线路。私营公司也进入了这个市场，其中一些是初创公司，背后的投资人相信需求会持续增长。好几个国家的政府提供了补贴，理由是夜车能帮它们实现减少交通排放的目标。

C The most frequently given explanation for this revival复兴，回潮 is concern about climate change. A passenger travelling by rail between two European cities is generally responsible for a small fraction一小部分 of the carbon dioxide produced by the same journey by air. Surveys suggest that a growing number of travellers, particularly younger people, feel uncomfortable about flying short distances. Yet environmental reasons are not the whole story. Many passengers say they choose the night train because it saves them a night in a hotel and delivers them to the city centre in the morning, ready to work or sightsee观光, without the long security queues and transfers that air travel involves.

【C 段译文】对这次复兴最常见的解释是人们对气候变化的担忧。在欧洲两个城市之间坐火车的乘客，产生的二氧化碳一般只有同一段路坐飞机的一小部分。调查显示，越来越多的旅客，特别是年轻人，对短途飞行感到不安。不过环保原因并不是全部。很多乘客说，他们选夜车是因为能省一晚酒店钱，而且早上直接到市中心，可以马上上班或观光，不用像坐飞机那样排长长的安检队、来回换乘。

D Running a night train, however, is far from simple. A sleeper carriage holds perhaps thirty passengers, compared with around eighty seats in a normal daytime carriage, so each ticket must earn more. The trains are used only once in twenty-four hours, and during the day the expensive carriages often stand idle闲置的 in sidings（铁路）侧线，停车轨. Operators must also pay track access charges线路使用费 to every country the train passes through, and these charges vary widely差别很大. In some countries they are set so high that, according to one operator, they account for nearly half the cost of a journey. Staff, too, must be trained to work under the different rules of each national rail system.

【D 段译文】不过，开夜车一点也不简单。一节卧铺车厢大概只能坐三十名乘客，而普通日间车厢大约有八十个座位，所以每张票必须赚更多钱。列车二十四小时里只用一次，白天这些昂贵的车厢常常闲置停在侧线上。运营方还得向列车经过的每个国家缴纳线路使用费，而各国收费差别很大。据一家运营商说，有些国家定得太高，几乎占到一趟行程成本的一半。员工也必须接受培训，才能按每个国家铁路系统的不同规定工作。

E Equipment is another obstacle障碍. Most of the sleeper carriages still in use were built in the 1970s and 1980s, and they are becoming difficult to maintain. Newer carriages are expensive and take years to design and build, because few manufacturers制造商 produce them. When Austria's railway ordered a new generation of sleepers, the first trains entered service more than a year later than planned. In the meantime在此期间, old carriages occasionally break down, and stories of cancelled trains, broken air-conditioning and missing dining cars spread quickly on social media.

【E 段译文】设备是另一个障碍。现在还在用的卧铺车厢大多造于 1970 和 1980 年代，越来越难维护。新车厢很贵，设计和制造都要好几年，因为生产它们的厂家很少。奥地利铁路订购新一代卧铺车时，第一批列车比计划晚了一年多才投入运营。在此期间，旧车厢时不时出故障，列车取消、空调坏了、餐车没了之类的经历在社交媒体上传得很快。

F Supporters argue that these difficulties are the result of policy rather than any basic weakness in the idea. They point out that aviation fuel航空燃油 is not taxed on international flights in Europe, whereas trains pay for the electricity they use, and that airports receive public money for infrastructure基础设施 in the same way railways do. If the costs of each form of transport reflected their real impact on the environment, they claim, night trains would be able to compete on price. Some go further and suggest that the European Union should create a single body to plan cross-border sleeper routes, rather than leaving each national operator to negotiate谈判，协商 separately.

【F 段译文】支持者认为，这些困难是政策造成的，而不是这个想法本身有什么根本缺陷。他们指出，在欧洲，国际航班的航空燃油不用交税，而火车要为用的电付钱；而且机场和铁路一样，也拿公共资金建基础设施。他们说，如果每种交通方式的成本都能反映它对环境的真实影响，夜车在价格上就能竞争。有人更进一步，建议欧盟成立一个统一机构来规划跨境卧铺线路，而不是让各国运营商各自去谈判。

G Whether night trains can become more than a niche小众的 product remains uncertain. Even their strongest supporters admit that they are unlikely to replace flights on very long routes, such as Lisbon to Stockholm, where the journey would take more than a day. Their natural market appears to be trips of between 800 and 1,500 kilometres, which are long enough to fill a night but short enough to arrive by breakfast. In my view, the revival is worth encouraging, but its success will depend less on romantic images of travel than on whether tickets can be bought easily and trains run reliably可靠地.

【G 段译文】夜车能不能不只是一个小众产品，现在还说不准。就连最坚定的支持者也承认，在里斯本到斯德哥尔摩这样超长的线路上，夜车不太可能取代飞机，因为路上要一天多。它的天然市场似乎是 800 到 1500 公里的行程：长到能填满一个晚上，又短到早饭前就能到。我的看法是，这次复兴值得鼓励，但成功与否，靠的不是浪漫的旅行想象，而是票好不好买、车能不能可靠准点地开。

During the day, the carriages are often left unused in (11) . Operators also pay track access (12) in every country on the route, and in some cases these make up almost half the cost of a trip. 原文：A sleeper carriage holds perhaps thirty passengers, compared with around eighty seats in a normal daytime carriage

2. G（出处：Paragraph G）

原文：they are unlikely to replace flights on very long routes, such as Lisbon to Stockholm, where the journey would take more than a day

not suitable 对应 unlikely to replace flights，例子是里斯本到斯德哥尔摩。

原文：the first trains entered service more than a year later than planned

late arrival of new trains = 新车比计划晚一年多投入运营。D 段也讲车厢但没讲交付延迟。

4. F（出处：Paragraph F）

原文：the European Union should create a single body to plan cross-border sleeper routes

proposal = suggest，new international organisation = a single body ... cross-border。

5. A（出处：Paragraph A）

原文：Germany's national operator ended its last overnight services, describing them as a business it could no longer afford

reason ... gave = describing them as ... could no longer afford，就是德国铁路给的理由。

6. YES（出处：Paragraph A）

原文：Low-cost airlines could carry people between capital cities in two hours for less than the price of a train ticket

cheap flights = low-cost airlines，作者把它列为卧铺消失的原因之一，所以 YES。

7. NOT GIVEN（出处：Paragraph B）

原文只说奥地利网络大，没比较各国乘客人数，NOT GIVEN。别因为奥地利出现得多就选 YES。

8. NO（出处：Paragraph C）

only reason 和 not the whole story 直接矛盾，NO。题干里有 only、always 这类绝对词要特别小心。

9. NO（出处：Paragraph G）

作者说最适合一晚能到的行程，超过一天的线路反而不适合，NO。

10. ticket（出处：Paragraph D）

原文：so each ticket must earn more

11. sidings（出处：Paragraph D）

track access 后面就是 charges，复数。

13. maintain（出处：Paragraph E）

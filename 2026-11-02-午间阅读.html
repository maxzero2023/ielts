The Surprising Story of the Pencil

A Some time in the 1560s, according to local legend, a violent storm uprooted连根拔起 a tree in Borrowdale, a remote偏远的 valley in the north-west of England. Among its roots, shepherds noticed a strange black substance物质 that left a dark, shiny mark on anything it touched. They soon began using lumps of it to mark their sheep, so that each farmer could identify his own flock（羊）群. At the time, nobody understood what the material really was. Because it looked rather like lead, people called it ‘black lead’, and this misunderstanding误解 explains why we still talk about pencil ‘lead’ today, even though a pencil has never contained any. The substance was, in fact, an unusually pure form of graphite, a type of carbon.

【A 段译文】据当地传说，大约在16世纪60年代，一场猛烈的暴风雨把英格兰西北部偏远山谷博罗代尔的一棵树连根拔起。牧羊人在树根之间发现了一种奇怪的黑色物质，它碰到什么都会留下一道深色、发亮的痕迹。他们很快就开始用一块块这种东西给羊做记号，好让每个农户认出自己的羊群。当时没人明白这种材料到底是什么。因为它看起来很像铅，人们就叫它“黑铅”。这个误解正好解释了为什么我们今天还把铅笔芯叫作“铅”，尽管铅笔里从来没有含过铅。这种物质其实是一种纯度极高的石墨，是碳的一种形态。

B It did not take long for the value of the Borrowdale deposit矿藏 to be recognised. Graphite was useful not only for writing but also for lining the moulds模具 in which cannonballs were made, which gave it military importance. As a result, the mine became one of the most closely guarded严密看守的 places in the country. It was opened for only a few weeks each year, and between these periods it was deliberately故意地 flooded so that thieves could not get in. Workers were searched at the end of every shift. Even so, smuggling走私 was widespread, and in 1752 Parliament passed a law that made stealing graphite a serious crime, punishable by可被处以 imprisonment or transportation to the colonies.

【B 段译文】没过多久，博罗代尔这处矿藏的价值就被人们认识到了。石墨不仅能用来写字，还能用来给铸造炮弹的模具做内衬，这让它有了军事上的重要性。因此，这座矿成了全国看守最严密的地方之一。它每年只开放几个星期，其余时间会被故意灌水淹没，让小偷进不去。工人每次下班都要被搜身。即便如此，走私仍然十分猖獗。1752年，议会通过了一项法律，把偷窃石墨定为重罪，可被判监禁或流放到殖民地。

C Graphite, however, has one obvious drawback缺点: it is soft and brittle易碎的；脆的, and it leaves marks on the hands of anyone who holds it. Early users solved this problem by wrapping sticks of it in string or sheepskin. A more elegant solution came from Italy, where craftsmen are thought to have hollowed out挖空 sticks of juniper wood and inserted the graphite inside. Later, carpenters in England and Germany developed a simpler method that is still used in principle today: two grooved带凹槽的 strips of wood are glued together around a thin rod of graphite, which is then cut and shaped.

【C 段译文】不过，石墨有一个明显的缺点：它又软又脆，谁拿着都会弄脏手。早期使用者的解决办法是用绳子或羊皮把石墨条包起来。更巧妙的办法来自意大利，据认为那里的工匠把杜松木条挖空，再把石墨塞进去。后来，英格兰和德国的木匠发明了一种更简单的方法，原理至今仍在使用：把两条带凹槽的木条粘合在一根细石墨棒外面，然后再切割成形。

D The next great breakthrough突破 was a result of conflict. In the 1790s France was at war with Britain and could no longer import graphite from Borrowdale. The French government asked Nicolas-Jacques Conté, an engineer and inventor, to find an alternative替代品. In 1795 he produced one: he mixed powdered graphite of lower quality with clay and water, formed the paste into thin rods, and fired them in a kiln窑. Conté discovered that by changing the proportion比例 of clay he could make the pencil harder or softer. This principle is the origin of the grading system printed on pencils today, in which H stands for ‘hard’ and B for ‘black’.

【D 段译文】下一个重大突破源于战争。18世纪90年代，法国与英国交战，再也无法从博罗代尔进口石墨。法国政府请工程师兼发明家尼古拉-雅克·孔泰想办法找到替代品。1795年他做到了：他把质量较差的石墨粉与黏土和水混合，把糊状物做成细棒，再放进窑里烧制。孔泰发现，改变黏土的比例就能让铅笔变硬或变软。这一原理就是今天铅笔上印的分级体系的起源，其中 H 代表“硬”，B 代表“黑”。

E In the nineteenth century, pencil making changed from a small-scale小规模的 craft into a major industry. The German city of Nuremberg became a centre of production, and some firms founded there still exist. In the United States, the family business of the writer Henry David Thoreau became known for making some of the best pencils in the country, partly thanks to improvements he made to the mixing process. New machines allowed factories to produce thousands of identical pencils a day, and in 1858 an American named Hymen Lipman received a patent专利 for a pencil with an eraser attached to one end, an idea that remains popular, particularly in North America.

【E 段译文】19世纪，铅笔制造从小规模手工业变成了一个大产业。德国城市纽伦堡成为生产中心，当年在那里创办的一些公司至今仍在经营。在美国，作家亨利·戴维·梭罗家的家族企业以生产全国最好的铅笔之一而闻名，部分原因是他改进了混料工艺。新机器让工厂每天能生产数千支一模一样的铅笔。1858年，一位名叫海曼·利普曼的美国人获得了一项专利，设计是在铅笔一端装上橡皮，这个点子至今仍很受欢迎，尤其是在北美。

F It might be expected that the computer would have made the pencil obsolete过时的；淘汰的, yet billions are still manufactured each year. Part of the reason is reliability可靠性. A pencil needs no battery, works in freezing temperatures and can even write upside down, unlike many pens. Its marks can be erased擦掉, which makes it ideal for sketches, crossword puzzles and multiple-choice exams. Carpenters and builders prefer it for marking measurements, while many artists value the range of tones色调 that different grades provide. Perhaps most importantly, the pencil is cheap and simple, which means that for millions of children around the world it is still the first tool they use to learn to write.

【F 段译文】人们也许以为电脑早就让铅笔过时了，可每年仍有数十亿支铅笔被生产出来。部分原因在于它可靠。铅笔不需要电池，在冰冻的气温下也能用，甚至可以倒着写，这一点很多笔都做不到。它的笔迹可以擦掉，所以特别适合画草图、做填字游戏和考选择题。木匠和建筑工人喜欢用它来标记尺寸，很多画家则看重不同等级铅笔能画出的丰富色调。也许最重要的是，铅笔便宜又简单，这意味着对全世界数以百万计的孩子来说，它仍然是他们学写字时用的第一样工具。

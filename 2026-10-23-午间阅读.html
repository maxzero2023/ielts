做题要求
  1. Section 3 是 G 类最长最难的一篇，今天三种题型：段落信息匹配（28–33）、特征匹配（34–37）、判断题 TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN（38–40）。
  2. 先花 3 分钟略读每段第一二句，在脑子里给每段一个小标题（A 起源，B 踏板，C 大前轮……）。
  3. 段落匹配：抓题目里的同义替换（比如 large-scale manufacturing 对应 mass production），字母可以重复用。
  4. 特征匹配：先在原文里定位大写的车名，再看它前后一两句；判断题注意 FALSE 是原文说反了，NOT GIVEN 是原文没说。
  5. 点“检查答案”，错题写错因：定位错 / 同义替换没认出 / 被干扰项带偏 / F 和 NG 分不清。最后点虚线词看中文，记 10 个生词到底部。

Text（Questions 28–40）

BICYCLES

A Forerunners先驱；前身 of the bicycle are depicted描绘 on ancient Greek tombstones墓碑 and in ancient Greek and Egyptian drawings. The first real bicycle is considered to be the CELERIFERE, a two-wheeled wooden horse designed by Comte Mede de Sivrac of France around 1790. The rider put his legs on either side of a horizontal bar attached to a wheel at either end, grasped the fixed handlebar, and propelled推动；推进 himself forward by pushing along the ground with alternate feet. Authorities专家；权威人士 disagree as to whether Sivrac ever built his CELERIFERE; in any event, despite a great deal of curious interest, it was no more than a popular toy. Probably the greatest change in two-wheeler design, perfected in 1818, came from a German, Baron Karl von Drais. His DRAISINE featured a steering bar attached to a spindle on the front wheel. A craze一时的狂热 for the bicycle developed among trend-setting引领潮流的 people in France and then in England, where it was called a ‘dandy horse’ or ‘hobby horse’.

B The first bicycle that could be propelled without the rider pushing against the ground with the feet was the MACMILLAN, invented by a Scots blacksmith铁匠 of the same name in 1839. He connected foot pedals to the rear wheel后轮 and also included a slightly improved handlebar and seat. The next important development of the bicycle came from two Frenchmen, Pierre Michaux and his son, Ernest, in 1861. Approached by a customer to repair his ‘dandy horse’, the Michaux changed its design instead. They fitted rotating pedals to the front axle车轴. This machine became known as the VELOCIPEDE and was quite popular. By 1865 the Michaux were producing 400 of them a year in their workshop. The smoothness of the VELOCIPEDE’s ride can be guessed at by its English nickname绰号, ‘boneshaker.’ A Michaux employee, Pierre Lallement, departed for the United States in 1866 when he was denied equal credit没得到同等的功劳认可 for the design of the VELOCIPEDE. With James Carrol of Connecticut, Lallement took out the first United States bicycle patent申请到美国第一个自行车专利 that year.

C The next 30 years saw many developments in the bicycle. The tubular metal bicycle frame was patented by an American called Madison in 1867. The French bicycle industry was stalled停滞 by the Franco-Prussian War of 1870, and England took the lead in bicycle development. Hard rubber tyres appeared in 1868, followed by the cycle lamp and crude简陋的 brakes. To improve speed, the front wheel gradually became larger. In 1870, James Starley of England designed a bicycle with a huge front wheel and very small rear wheel. Formally named the ARIEL, Starley’s machine was commonly called the ‘penny-farthing’, after two very different-sized British coins of the time. Also called a ‘high’ or ‘ordinary’, the bicycle was both easier to pedal and faster but was very unstable不稳的 because of its high centre of gravity重心. Applying the brakes was likely to propel the rider head-first over the handlebars – hence its nickname, ‘bonebreaker.’

D The hazards危险 of the ‘penny-farthing’ led to two new developments. One was the TRICYCLE, which became quite popular with female cyclists. The other innovation was the ‘safety’ bicycle built by an Englishman in 1876. The ‘safety’ had two medium-sized wheels of approximately equal diameter直径 and a chain-driven rear wheel. Mass production大规模生产 of the bicycle was initiated开始；发起 with the ‘Rover safety bicycle’ in 1884 by John Kemp, nephew of the ‘penny-farthing’ inventor. With the advent出现；到来 of the diamond-shaped metal frame, the structure of the modern bicycle was essentially in place. The only thing that was missing was the pneumatic充气的 (air-filled) tyre, and this was invented in 1888.

E The ensuing随之而来的 wave of popularity for bicycle riding had several important social effects. Women had, for the first time, an acceptable, healthy form of exercise that also promoted sensible changes in restrictive束缚的；限制性的 female clothing. The public gained an inexpensive means of increased mobility出行能力；流动性. And in the United States, bicycling gave birth to a demand for greatly improved roads – facilitating促进 the development of the automobile.

F By the beginning of the 20th century, the bicycle had effective braking systems, electric lights, and multiple speed gearing mechanisms. It also had the advantage of costing less than a month’s wages. Improvements continued to be made between 1919 and 1945, especially in Europe, where the bicycle was very popular, with advances being made in inflatable tyre technology and in the development of lightweight components零部件. In America, however, in these years, bicycle popularity declined to the point where it was only regarded as a child’s toy. In 1933, Schwinn produced an impractical, single-speed bike whose wide tyres made it resemble像；类似 a motorcycle. Despite its rugged结实粗犷的 and contemporary appearance, it was both too noisy and far too costly for the average worker to buy. After 1945, bike sales plummeted暴跌 in Europe but began to rise in America as the lightweight, derailleur-gear bikes began to be imported from Europe.

G By the 1960s, the ROAD BIKE, built for speed with its thin tyres and drop handlebars, became very popular. In the mid-1970s, designers cleverly combined steel alloy合金 frames using new lightweight technology and old-fashioned balloon tyres and invented the MOUNTAIN BIKE. In 1986, bicycle sales in the United States increased by 80 percent, with MOUNTAIN BIKES rising to account for占（比例） a 35 percent share. As the present century progresses, bicycles will have super-light but strong carbon-fibre composite frames, motorcycle-style brakes, electronic transmission, and even electronically-assisted pedalling. Shock absorbers will become standard, as will titanium pedals and Kevlar tyres. As the world total of bicycles approaches nearly one billion, people are using bicycles for sport, touring, transport, fitness, and even as a way to get back to nature.

Questions 38–40

Do the following statements agree with the information given in the text? TRUE 与原文一致，FALSE 与原文相反，NOT GIVEN 原文没提到。

  1. In 19th century England, increasing the size of the front wheel improved stability.
  2. In the 1960s, bicycle production in the US increased.
  3. In 1986, more than half the bicycles sold in the US were mountain bikes.

答案与解析（先做完再点开）

28. F（出处：F 段）

原文：Despite its rugged and contemporary appearance, it was both too noisy and far too costly for the average worker to buy.

普通工人买不起 = 只有有钱人买得起。注意 F 段开头还说自行车不到一个月工资，别被它带偏，题目说的是 1933 年 Schwinn 那款。

29. E（出处：E 段）

原文：…bicycling gave birth to a demand for greatly improved roads – facilitating the development of the automobile.

transport infrastructure 交通基础设施 = roads 道路。

30. D（出处：D 段）

原文：With the advent of the diamond-shaped metal frame, the structure of the modern bicycle was essentially in place. The only thing that was missing was the pneumatic (air-filled) tyre…

completed the design 对应 structure … in place 和最后补上的充气轮胎。

31. C（出处：C 段）

原文：Formally named the ARIEL, Starley’s machine was commonly called the ‘penny-farthing’, after two very different-sized British coins of the time.

一大一小两个轮子像一大一小两枚硬币，名字来自外形。B 段 boneshaker 是因为骑着颠，不是外观，别选错。

32. D（出处：D 段）

原文：Mass production of the bicycle was initiated with the ‘Rover safety bicycle’ in 1884…

large-scale manufacturing = mass production，first = initiated。NB 说字母可重复用，30 和 32 都是 D。

33. G（出处：G 段）

原文：…designers cleverly combined steel alloy frames using new lightweight technology and old-fashioned balloon tyres and invented the MOUNTAIN BIKE.

skilful = cleverly，earlier style = old-fashioned balloon tyres，new metal technology = steel alloy frames using new lightweight technology。

34. B（出处：B 段）

原文：The smoothness of the VELOCIPEDE’s ride can be guessed at by its English nickname, ‘boneshaker.’

boneshaker 骨头都颠散了，反话说明一点不平稳 = very bumpy ride。

35. C（出处：C 段）

原文：…despite a great deal of curious interest, it was no more than a popular toy.

no more than a toy = only used for fun。

36. F（出处：F 段）

原文：The first bicycle that could be propelled without the rider pushing against the ground with the feet was the MACMILLAN…

以前用脚蹬地，它第一次用踏板，改变了让车动起来的方式。

37. A（出处：A 段）

原文：A craze for the bicycle developed among trend-setting people in France and then in England…

trend-setting people = fashionable people，craze = became popular。D 选项其实说的是 penny-farthing 刹车会把人甩出去，题里没有它，是干扰项。

38. FALSE（出处：C 段）

原文：To improve speed, the front wheel gradually became larger… but was very unstable because of its high centre of gravity.

前轮变大是为了速度，结果反而很不稳，和题目“提高了稳定性”相反，所以 FALSE。

39. NOT GIVEN（出处：G 段）

原文：By the 1960s, the ROAD BIKE, built for speed with its thin tyres and drop handlebars, became very popular.

原文只说 60 年代公路车流行，没说美国产量增加，所以 NOT GIVEN。popular 不等于 production increased。

40. FALSE（出处：G 段）

原文：In 1986, bicycle sales in the United States increased by 80 percent, with MOUNTAIN BIKES rising to account for a 35 percent share.

山地车只占 35%，不到一半，和 more than half 矛盾，FALSE。80% 是销量增幅，是干扰数字。
